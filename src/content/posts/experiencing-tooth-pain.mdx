Tooth pain is a problem that most people will have to deal with at some point in their lives. But just because it is common doesn’t mean that it should be ignored.

### Are You Suffering From Tooth Pain?

Tooth pain can be an indicator of **a much larger problem**, and the longer it goes untreated, the worse it will get. Learn about the top 6 reasons you may be experiencing tooth pain, and find out what you can do to relieve and prevent it.

### 6 Common Tooth Pain Causes

Tooth pain can be caused by a number of factors, but these are the most common.

-   **Tooth Decay –** As the outer and inner layers of your teeth decay, the nerves inside becoming exposed, which can lead to sharp pain and sensitivity.
-   **Abscessed Tooth –** An abscessed tooth is an infection at the base or root of the tooth. It can cause a gnawing or throbbing kind of tooth pain
-   **Tooth Fracture –** Fractured teeth expose the nerves inside. If the fracture is severe, the pain can be quite severe too.
-   **Damaged Filling –** Damaged fillings can expose or rub against the nerves in the tooth, creating occasional pain, or severe pain
-   **Repetitive Motion –** Motions, like eating ice cubes or grinding your teeth, can wear down the protective outer layer of the teeth and expose the sensitive inner layer
-   **Infected Gums –** Since the teeth and gums are directly connected, an infection in the gums can feel to the patient like tooth pain.

### **Ways to Relieve Tooth Pain**

If you experience occasional or mild tooth pain, there are some effective home remedies you can use to relieve the discomfort.

-   **Clove Oil –** mix 2-3 drops clove oil and ¼ teaspoon olive oil and soak a cotton ball in the mixture. Place the cotton ball on the site of the pain.
-   **Cough Drop –** Cough drops contain a minor anesthetic that can help relieve minor tooth pain.
-   **Floss –** Trapped food can cause tooth pain. Use floss to get it out.
-   **Hot Tea Bag –** Tea contains tannic acid which can reduce swelling. Place the bag at the site of the pain.

### **When to See a Dentist about Tooth Pain**

Some tooth pain you may be able to relieve yourself, but if the condition is serious, you will need to **let a dentist examine your teeth**. Make an appointment if you experience any of these symptoms.

-   Your tooth pain lasts longer than 1-2 days
-   You tooth pain is acute or severe
-   Your mouth hurts when you open wide
-   Your tooth pain is accompanied by a fever or an earache

#### Proper Oral Health is the Best Prevention for Tooth Pain

How can you prevent tooth pain from appearing in the first place? Simple, just **brush and floss daily**, and see a **dentist regularly**. Making proper oral health a daily priority is by far the best preventative medicine.
