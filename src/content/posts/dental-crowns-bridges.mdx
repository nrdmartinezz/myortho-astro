### Fix Your Broken, Cracked, or Missing Teeth

A person’s smile is one of the first things you notice about his or her appearance. People with a big, bright smile are naturally magnetic and personable. Unfortunately, if you have broken, cracked, or missing teeth, you might be reluctant to show off one of your biggest and best features. The good news is that you don’t have to hide your smile if you take steps to restore it. **Learn how dental crowns and bridges can transform your smile and your whole image with it.**

#### What are Dental Crowns and Bridges?

Dental crowns and bridges are two different but related remedies. [Dental crowns](/contact/) cover up teeth that are damaged by cracks, breaks, or discoloration. The original tooth is underneath a sculpted mold that mimics the look of a perfect tooth. [Dental bridges](/contact/) are used to fill in the gaps when you have a missing tooth. A carefully molded tooth occupies the missing spot(s) giving you an uninterrupted row of teeth.

#### What is Involved?

Both of these procedures require several trips to the dentist. The process begins with an assessment of your damaged teeth and preparation of the area where the crown or bridge will be installed. During the second visit, the crown or bridge will be permanently put in place. You will typically then come in for a follow-up visit to make sure everything is working as planned.

#### Does It Hurt?

When the crown or bridge is installed, the area is completely numb. Working with **an experienced doctor ensures that this procedure is as pain-free** as possible. There can be some minor discomfort in the days and weeks following the procedure, but this has more to do with the feeling of something new in the mouth than the experience of pain. The vast majority of patients quickly get used to their dental crown or bridge.

#### Is this a Permanent Solution?

When properly cared for, dental crowns and bridges can be a permanent solution. **The best way to care for them is to follow a diligent oral care routine** that includes daily brushing and flossing as well as regular trips to the dentist. It is possible to damage the crown or bridge if you eat the wrong foods or fail to take care of your teeth. Poor oral health can also lead to new damaged teeth and require further procedures.
