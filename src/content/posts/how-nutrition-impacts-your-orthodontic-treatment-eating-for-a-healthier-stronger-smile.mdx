When you begin your orthodontic care at **MyOrthodontist** in Fayetteville, NC, we often talk about foods to avoid—think sticky candies, crunchy snacks, and anything that could harm your braces or clear aligners. While steering clear of these treats is essential, what you *choose* to eat plays an equally vital role in maintaining your oral health and helping your treatment progress smoothly.

### Building Blocks for Strong Teeth and Healthy Gums

Teeth and the bones supporting them are living tissues that thrive on a balanced intake of nutrients. One of the most critical minerals is **calcium**—the cornerstone of tooth enamel and jawbone strength. You’ll find calcium in dairy products like milk and cheese, but also in plant-based options such as kale, bok choy, almonds, and fortified non-dairy milks.

Alongside calcium, **phosphorus** is indispensable. It teams up with calcium to rebuild and reinforce enamel. Foods rich in phosphorus include lean meats, fish, eggs, and legumes. Another key player is **Vitamin D**—without it, your body can’t absorb calcium properly. Fortunately, Fayetteville’s sunny days help, and you can also get Vitamin D from fatty fish and fortified foods.

Don’t overlook **Vitamin C**, which is crucial for producing collagen—the protein that keeps your gums resilient and supports the periodontal ligament that anchors your teeth. Vitamin C deficiencies can lead to weaker gum tissue and increase the risk of gum disease. Load up on citrus fruits, strawberries, bell peppers, and broccoli to keep your gums healthy.

### Foods to Limit: Protecting Your Enamel from Damage

Just as important as knowing what to eat is understanding what to limit. **Sugar** feeds the bacteria in your mouth that produce acids, which wear down enamel and cause cavities. This is especially important during orthodontic treatment, as braces and wires can trap food, making thorough cleaning more challenging.

**Acidic beverages** such as sodas, energy drinks, sports drinks, and some fruit juices can directly erode tooth enamel, regardless of sugar content. This process, known as acid erosion, weakens your teeth and can lead to sensitivity and decay. Drinking plenty of water—preferably fluoridated—is one of the simplest and most effective ways to protect your smile during treatment.

### Simple Nutritional Tips for Orthodontic Patients

| Nutrient | Why It Matters | Best Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calcium | Strengthens enamel and jawbone | Dairy, leafy greens, almonds, fortified milks |
| Phosphorus | Works with calcium to rebuild enamel | Lean meats, fish, eggs, legumes |
| Vitamin D | Enhances calcium absorption | Sunlight, fatty fish, fortified foods |
| Vitamin C | Supports gum health and collagen | Citrus, strawberries, bell peppers, broccoli |
| Fluoride | Helps protect enamel from acid | Fluoridated water, fluoride toothpaste |
