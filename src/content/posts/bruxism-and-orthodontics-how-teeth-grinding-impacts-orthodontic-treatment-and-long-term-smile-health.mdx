## What Is Bruxism and Why Is It Important?

Bruxism is the unconscious grinding or clenching of teeth, most often occurring during sleep but sometimes during waking hours as a response to stress or concentration. While occasional clenching is common, chronic bruxism can place excessive force on your teeth, jaw joints, and orthodontic appliances.

Over time, untreated grinding can contribute to tooth wear, jaw discomfort, headaches, and complications that interfere with orthodontic treatment.

### Common Signs of Bruxism

-   Morning headaches or facial soreness
-   Jaw pain or tightness
-   Worn, flattened, or chipped teeth
-   Tooth sensitivity
-   Popping or clicking in the jaw joints
-   Broken brackets, bent wires, or damaged dental work

If you notice any of these symptoms, our team can evaluate your bite and develop a treatment strategy tailored to your needs.

## How Bruxism Can Affect Orthodontic Treatment

Orthodontic treatment relies on carefully controlled forces to guide teeth into their ideal positions. Teeth grinding introduces excessive pressure that can interfere with this process and sometimes prolong treatment.

| Effect of Bruxism | Impact During Treatment | Potential Long-Term Consequences |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Excessive pressure on teeth | Can interfere with controlled tooth movement | Extended treatment times |
| Damage to braces or appliances | Broken brackets and bent wires may require repairs | Additional appointments and delays |
| Jaw muscle strain | Discomfort may affect appliance wear | Persistent TMJ symptoms |
| Tooth wear and fractures | Complicates alignment and bite correction | Weakened enamel and restorative needs |
| Joint inflammation | May require treatment modifications | Chronic jaw pain and dysfunction |

At MyOrthodontist, we carefully monitor patients throughout treatment and make adjustments when signs of bruxism are present. Early intervention helps protect both your appliances and your final results.

## Managing Bruxism During Orthodontic Care

Fortunately, teeth grinding doesn’t have to derail your smile journey. Our individualized approach allows us to customize solutions that support successful treatment and long-term oral health.

### Custom Nightguards

For many patients, a custom-fitted nightguard provides an effective way to cushion the teeth and absorb grinding forces during sleep. These appliances are designed to work alongside orthodontic treatment when appropriate and help minimize damage to both teeth and appliances.

### Stress Management and Lifestyle Support

Stress is a common contributor to bruxism. Relaxation techniques, exercise, meditation, and healthy sleep habits may help reduce clenching and grinding episodes. We believe exceptional care extends beyond the mouth and includes supporting your overall well-being.

### Treatment Modifications

Depending on your needs, our orthodontists may make adjustments to your braces or aligners to help minimize excessive forces and keep your treatment progressing efficiently.

## Protecting Your Smile After Treatment

The end of active orthodontic treatment isn’t the end of caring for your smile. Patients who grind their teeth may be at greater risk for relapse, tooth wear, or damage to retainers if bruxism remains unmanaged.

That’s why retention and long-term monitoring are essential. Wearing retainers as instructed and maintaining regular dental visits help preserve the beautiful smile you’ve worked so hard to achieve.
