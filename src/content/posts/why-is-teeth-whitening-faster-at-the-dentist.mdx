### The Low-Down on Tooth Stains

At school or in a business meeting, one of the first things many people notice is our teeth. Smiles help connect us to others, and having white teeth can be an important part of making a good first impression. Unfortunately, there are a number of things that can change or alter the color of your teeth, and many of these are commonly found in our everyday routines.

Many foods, from your morning cup of coffee to a relaxing glass of wine, can cause teeth to become more vulnerable to staining and can cause some discoloration because of their intense color and acidic properties. Other things, like damage to the teeth as a result of trauma, can also incite color changes that may be very difficult to fix with normal oral hygiene alone. Additionally, our teeth can also change color over time as a natural part of the aging process, although the specific changes and how impactful they are will differ from person to person.

### The Problem of Teeth Whitening at Home

#### Limited Purchase Options

The first thing that many people try is at-home whitening products that can be found at your local pharmacy or grocery store. These products include things like toothpaste, strips, or trays, and people enjoy the idea of being able to use these without ever setting foot in the dentist’s office. Unfortunately, this idea can often lead to disappointment and mixed results.

#### Everyone’s Using Them

The availability of these products can sometimes make them seem like they are the best option for the average person, but in reality this is rarely the case. Since at-home whitening products are mass-produced and widely available to everyone, how effective they actually are varies a lot from person-to-person and product-to-product. Without guidance, finding out which of these options is best can be a difficult and expensive ordeal.

Furthermore, the improper use of some at-home treatments can lead to issues like tooth sensitivity and gum irritation. Worst of all, it can take a very long time to see any results, and sometimes, you may not see any satisfactory results at all.

#### Slow and Unreliable

In many situations, whitening your smile is not as straightforward as it might seem at first glance. The strength of store-bought whitening solutions can be impossible to know or can even change from batch-to-batch. This can slow your progress and make it seem like no matter what you do, your teeth are seemingly resistant to improving.

Another issue that can complicate things is that some dental conditions may make it more difficult to see results. One example of these is tartar. Tartar, or the yellowish bone-colored material that can form on a tooth over time, can actually end up covering your teeth and creating a solid structure that does not allow the whitening gel or other materials to actually work on the tooth itself.

Visiting the dentist and getting help from a dental professional will remove a lot of the guesswork, and in the end, this is what makes in-office treatments much faster and more effective than just trying it out yourself.
